**[COMMON CASE USES]{.ul}**

**[Nominative Case]{.ul}** \[Smyth 938-975\]

-   Subject of a conjugated verb

-   Predicative Nominative = the substantive after a copular verb \["to
    be" or "to become"\] or verbs that function like copular verbs \["I
    feel [ill]{.ul}" or "You seem [sleepy]{.ul}"\] whose subject is in
    the nominative.

**[Genitive Case]{.ul}** \[Smyth 1289-1449\]

-   Possession \["[Xerxes']{.ul} army"\]

-   Separation/source (with or without a preposition) \["they left [from
    Miletos]{.ul}"\]

-   Time within which \["[at night]{.ul}"\] and place within which
    \["they marched [in the plain]{.ul}"\]

-   Partitive/Genitive of the whole \["one [of the soldiers]{.ul}"\]

-   Material \["a mountains of money" or "born [of a good
    family]{.ul}"\]

    -   *Note: often overlaps with partitive!*

-   Quality/description \["a woman [of great wisdom]{.ul}"\]

-   Measure \["a width [of ten feet]{.ul}"\]

-   Subjective and objective genitive

    -   Subjective is possession \["love [of the father]{.ul}" = the
        father is loving someone\]

    -   Objective \["love [of the father]{.ul}" = the father is being
        loved by someone\]

-   Value/price \["a bribe [of 60 drachmas]{.ul}"\]

-   Genitive object after verbs of

    -   sensory perception \[hearing, touching, enjoying, etc.\]

    -   beginning and ruling \["rule [Macedonia]{.ul}\]

    -   aiming at, striving for \["aiming [for glory]{.ul}"\]

    -   remembering and forgetting \["being mindful [of the
        danger]{.ul}"\]

    -   filling \["being full [of water]{.ul}"\]

    -   emotion (the genitive indicates the cause) \["She was grieved
        [by his behavior]{.ul}"\]

-   Criminal charge \["he was accused [of murder]{.ul}"\]

-   After verbs compounding with a preposition that normally takes the
    genitive

-   Comparison \["He is taller than [me]{.ul}"\]

**[Dative Case]{.ul}** \[Smyth 1450-1550\]

*Note: the old Indo-European Instrumental and Locative cases are thrown
into the Dative in Greek*.

-   Indirect object and related datives:

    -   Interest (*the person for whom something is done*)

    -   Advantage/disadvantage \["They went to war [for Athens]{.ul}"\]

    -   Reference (the person whose opinion it is \["It seems [to
        me]{.ul} that..."\]

-   Place where/locative \["[in Athens]{.ul}"\]

-   Time when \["at dawn"\]

-   Instrument/means \["He wrapped himself [in a blanket]{.ul}"\]

-   Manner \["She graduated [with distinction]{.ul}"\]

-   Cause \["They waged war [out of necessity]{.ul}"\]

-   Degree of Difference after comparisons \["longer [by a mile]{.ul}"\]

-   Dative object after verbs of:

    -   helping, injuring, blaming, threatening, etc.

    -   yielding and obeying

    -   comparing \[similar [to this]{.ul}"\]

    -   accompaniment \["He came [with them]{.ul}"\]

    -   Also verbs compounded with prepositions that take the dative

-   Possession (*the person for whom something exists*) \["the book is
    [to me]{.ul}"\]

-   Agent (only after passive verbs) \["It was accomplished [by
    them]{.ul}"\]

**[Accusative Case]{.ul}** \[Smyth 1551-1635\]

-   Direct object

-   Place to which

-   Extent of space over which an action happens \["they marched [six
    miles]{.ul}"\]

-   Extent of time \["they sailed [for three days]{.ul}\]

-   Respect \["He was injured [in the head]{.ul}"\]

-   Adverbial accusative \["He arrived in the agora [in a timely
    fashion]{.ul}" or "[in the end]{.ul}, she was correct"\]

    -   *Note: almost any adjective can be thrown into the neuter
        accusative and it becomes an adverb*

-   Some verbs take two accusative objects \["He asked [him]{.ul} [a
    question]{.ul}."

**[Vocative Case]{.ul}** \[Smyth 1283-1288\]

-   Direct address \["O [Athenians]{.ul}, I promise I am innocent."\]
