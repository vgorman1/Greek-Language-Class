1.2 Nouns

Gold Standard translations and commentary

1.  They set up a constitution.

NOTE: κατίστημι has many meanings, depending on the context. Its root
word, ἵστημι, means "stand" or "set," so κατίστημι literally means
something like "set down" or "set x up." Most commonly it can mean
anything from "station" to "arrange" to "organize" to "appoint."

2.  Hippokrates, the governor, led away the soldiers.

NOTE: The prefix ἐκ/ἐξ = "out of."

3.  Thrasyboulos and the citizens were coming to the aid of the
    government.

4.  Dorieus, the son of Diagoros, was sailing out.

NOTE: It is very common to follow a proper name with the father's name
in the genitive as a patronymic. Literally, "Dorieus of Diagoros."
Athenians also often follow the proper name with an adjective indicating
one's deme, the village in Attica whence the family arose and therefore
the person's place of enrollment as a citizen.

5.  The Athenians sailed with their army.

NOTE: Whereas στατόπεδον usually means "encampment," it can also be used
as a synonym for the army itself.

6.  Lysander took Philokles of the generals.

7.  Lysander led away the hoplites.

8.  Lysander was sending away the guard of the Athenians.

NOTE: Without a larger context it is impossible to know whether τῶν
Ἀθηναίων is a subjective (possessive = the guard belongs to the
Athenians) or an objective genitive (it is a guard that has been set
over the Athenians.).

9.  Lysander sent Aristotle to the ephors.

NOTE: Ἀθηναῖον = ethnic identifier.

10. They yielded.

NOTE: Literally, "give away." Used intransitively (without an OBJ) it
means "yield."

11. They made Peisander naurarch.

12. Thrasyllos led away the army.

13. The Peloponnesians fled.

NOTE: Literally, "A flight of the Peloponnesians occurred."

14. Thrasyllos sent away the prisoners.

15. The Athenians were besieging Byzantion.

16. The multitude was shouting.

17. He awarded prizes to the peltasts and the archers.

> NOTE: προτίθημι means, literally, "set before." Often prizes were
> formally announced before contests began.

18. They chose the encampment site.

19. He was in possession of horses for the army.

> NOTE: Dative of Interest.

20. He was asking for pay for the sailors.

> NOTE: ναὺτης is another masculine first declension noun, meaning
> sailor.
