Instructions for Making a Gold Standard Tree File

> Once you make your own dependency syntax trees, you can compare them
> to trees I have made and find out where we differ. Remember that, just
> because something is marked wrong does not necessarily mean that it is
> wrong. The key could contain mistakes and, more importantly, there are
> often multiple correct answers, since language is a complex function
> that is often ambiguous, especially when it occurs out of context.
> Think about the "mistakes" and decide whether they are truly wrong or
> whether they are reasonable renderings based on your knowledge of
> grammar and syntax. For example, whether a given word is labelled AuxY
> or AuxZ may make very little difference, or, likewise, where you hang
> the "not" in a given clause.

-   Download the GS (Gold Standard) file from the link onto your hard
    drive.

    -   "Feedback" is self-graded practice. The files are in the chapter
        module, along with translations and embedded commentary.

    -   Anything labelled "graded" or "test" is an assignment. The file
        is linked under the assignment instructions for that week

-   Upload the GS file into Perseids, using NEW TREEBANK ANNOTATION \>
    "Choose file" \[at the bottom of the page\]

-   Make your trees. SAVE often!

-   Go to the Sentence List page for that file. Use CHANGE TITLE to
    rename it to something that make sense, so you can find it again

-   The key is linked in for you. After you have made your trees, go to
    the Sentence List page for that file. OVERVIEW \> COMPARE \[very
    bottom of page\]. Once in your individual sentences, click on the
    REVIEW tab \[far right\] \> COMPARE. Mistakes will appear in red.
