Subordinate Clauses in Greek

I.  TEMPORAL CLAUSES \[Smyth 2383-2461\]

    A.  When, relative to the main verb?

        1.  Same time as main verb

            a.  *When*? ὅτε, ὁπότε, ἡνίκα

            b.  *While* ἕως

        2.  Prior to

            a.  *After/when* ἐπεί, ἐπείδη

        3.  Subsequent to

            a.  *Until* ἕως, μέχρι

            b.  *Before* πρίν \[takes the indicative when the principal
                clause is negative; infinitive when positive\]

    B.  Present and past temporal clauses take the indicative mood when
        the action is marked as a fact and refers to a definite
        occasion.

    C.  Future temporal clauses take the subjunctive or optative with
        ἄν.

II. CAUSAL \[2240-2248\]

    A.  ὅτι, ἐπεί, ἐπείδη, ὅτε, ὡς = because, since

        1.  Neg = οὐ

        2.  Fact = indicative

III. PURPOSE CLAUSES \[FINAL CLAUSES\] \[Smyth 2193-2206\]

     A.  ἵνα, ὅπως, ὡς

     B.  Negated by μή

     C.  Subjunctive after primary tenses

     D.  Optative after secondary tenses

IV. OBJECT CLAUSES

    A.  Of EFFORT \[Smyth 2209-2219\]

        1.  *to take care, take pains, strive*

        2.  ὅπως, negated by μή

        3.  Take future indicative for the most part

    B.  Of FEARING \[Smyth 2221-2239\]

        1.  Introduces by μή (*lest*) or μὴ οὐ (*lest not*)

        2.  Subjunctive after primary tenses

        3.  Optative after secondary tenses

        4.  Fear about the present or past takes indicative

V.  RESULT \[2249-2278\]

    A.  ὥστε = so that

        1.  Often refers back to a demonstrative adverb/adjective in the
            main clause: οὕτως (*thus*) τοιοῦτος (such), τοσοῦτος (*so
            great*)

        2.  Usually takes the infinitive to explain the main thought
            with an anticipated or possible result

    B.  Rarely takes the indicative to explain an actual result.

VI. COMPARISON CLAUSES \[2462-2487\]

    A.  Take indicative for statements of fact

    B.  ὠς, ὅπως (as), ὥσπερ, καθάπερ (just as)

    C.  Principal clause often contains a demonstrative adverb

VII. Some Conditionals

     A.  Present Simple εἰ + pr/pf indic + pr/pf indic

         1.  A simple assertion with no indication of its reality or
             probability. A truism.

         2.  If he buys a boat, he can trade more easily.

     B.  Past Simple εἰ + impf/aor/pp indic + impf/aor/pp indic

         1.  If he bought a boat, he was smart.

     C.  Present Unreal εἰ + impf indic + impf indic + ἄν

         1.  An unreal assertion

         2.  ἄν

         3.  If he were buying a boat, he would have an excuse for being
             late.

     D.  Past Unreal εἰ + impf/aor indic + aor/impf indic + ἄν

         1.  If he had bought a boat, he would be home by now
